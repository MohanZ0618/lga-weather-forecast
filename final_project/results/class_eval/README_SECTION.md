## Model evaluation checks (Lecture 12)

`python final_project/class_eval.py` (about 100 seconds, offline, 2 threads) applies learning curves, cross-validation, regularization and overfitting checks to the selected model (CURRENT: censored Gaussian with HRRR plus LAMP/NBM, trained 2021–2023). Every choice still uses validation years only. It reproduces CURRENT's test log loss (0.1995 / 0.1572 / 0.0722) and refits the paper's selected classifiers to within 0.0012 of the saved paper scores. Outputs, including `learning_curves.png` and `regularization_path.png`, are in `final_project/results/class_eval/`. `test_class_eval.py` checks that no fold trains on its own year or a later one.

- **Learning curves.** We trained on the most recent N days with hyperparameters held fixed. CURRENT's 2024 validation log loss barely moves from 400 to 1040 days (0.209 to 0.211 at 09:00, 0.173 to 0.172 at 12:00, 0.094 to 0.098 at 15:00), and its training loss is about the same (0.221, 0.175, 0.080). So the model is limited by bias, or by the noise these inputs leave, not by variance. More data from the same years would barely help: gains have to come from better inputs or a better model form. Quantile boosting on the same inputs keeps a training-validation gap (0.045, 0.059, 0.021), which is the sign of variance. With observations only and 2010–2023 history, the Gaussian stays flat from 1040 to 5108 days, while boosting keeps improving (0.426 to 0.412, 0.287 to 0.255, 0.147 to 0.131). Boosting needs 1040, 3500 and 5108 days to catch the Gaussian on validation, and on test it is ahead only at 09:00. The flexible model is data-hungry and the linear one is data-efficient. Points below 365 days also lose whole seasons of training data, not just sample size.
- **Rolling-origin cross-validation** (train 2021, validate 2022; train 2021–2022, validate 2023; train 2021–2023, validate 2024) agrees with the single 2024 split at 09:00. At 12:00 it picks a stronger penalty (L2 10 instead of 0.01) and, for the model choice, SHASH instead of the Gaussian. Neither test change can be told apart from zero (−0.002 [−0.008, +0.004] and +0.002 [−0.005, +0.009]). At 15:00 it avoids the SHASH model that the 2024 split preferred (test 0.0792 to 0.0743, −0.005 [−0.009, −0.0004]), but its own L2 choice of 3 costs +0.002 [+0.0002, +0.004] against 10. The early folds have only 346 training days and score worse (CURRENT 0.289 on 2022 against 0.211 on 2024), so the CV average mixes how hard each year was with how much data each fold had.
- **Regularization path.** Validation log loss is flat for L2 from 0.01 to 10. It climbs steeply from L2 = 100 (0.246 at 09:00, reaching 0.493 at 1000) because the penalty shrinks the coefficients too far, which is underfitting. There is no overfitting side: with about 50 coefficients and 1040 days, the nearly unpenalized fit already generalizes. At 15:00 the test score happens to be lowest at L2 = 100 (0.069), where validation says the penalty is too strong; we keep the validation choice.
- **Overfitting check.** CURRENT's train, validation and test log losses are close: 0.221 / 0.211 / 0.200 at 09:00, 0.175 / 0.172 / 0.157 at 12:00, and 0.080 / 0.098 / 0.072 at 15:00. The paper's selected two-classifier average scores 0.152 / 0.120 / 0.053 on training days against 0.264 / 0.238 / 0.134 on validation, a gap of 0.08 to 0.12. Distance-weighted KNN memorizes its training days (training log loss 0.003 to 0.004, validation 0.27 to 0.42). XGBoost and random forest have gaps of 0.07 to 0.18. Gaussian naive Bayes has a small gap but a large loss everywhere (0.27 to 0.83 on training days), the textbook high-bias case.
