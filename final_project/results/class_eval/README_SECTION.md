## Model evaluation checks (Lecture 12)

`python final_project/class_eval.py` (about 100 seconds, offline, 2 threads) applies learning curves, cross-validation, regularization and overfitting checks to the selected model (CURRENT: censored Gaussian with HRRR plus LAMP/NBM, trained 2021–2023). Every choice still uses validation years only. It reproduces CURRENT's test log loss (0.1818 / 0.1358 / 0.0701) and refits the paper's selected classifiers with probabilities identical to the saved ones. Outputs, including `learning_curves.png` and `regularization_path.png`, are in `final_project/results/class_eval/`. `test_class_eval.py` checks that no fold trains on its own year or a later one.

- **Learning curves.** We trained on the most recent N days with hyperparameters held fixed. CURRENT's 2024 validation log loss does not improve from 400 to 1040 days (0.190 to 0.187 at 09:00, 0.160 to 0.169 at 12:00, 0.077 to 0.082 at 15:00), and its training loss is about the same (0.210, 0.173, 0.077). So the model is limited by bias, or by the noise these inputs leave, not by variance. More data from the same years would barely help: gains have to come from better inputs or a better model form. Quantile boosting on the same inputs keeps a training-validation gap (0.033, 0.020, 0.012) and is still improving at 1040 days at 09:00 and 15:00, which is the sign of variance; at 15:00 it already edges the Gaussian on validation (0.077 against 0.082). With observations only and 2010–2023 history, the Gaussian stays flat from 1040 to 5108 days, while boosting keeps improving (0.423 to 0.417, 0.283 to 0.261, 0.117 to 0.100). Boosting needs 700, 3500 and 2000 days to pass the Gaussian on validation, and on test it is ahead only at 09:00. The flexible model is data-hungry and the linear one is data-efficient. Points below 365 days also lose whole seasons of training data, not just sample size.
- **Rolling-origin cross-validation** (train 2021, validate 2022; train 2021–2022, validate 2023; train 2021–2023, validate 2024) agrees with the single 2024 split at 09:00 (CURRENT, L2 10). At 12:00 it picks a stronger penalty (L2 10 instead of 0.1) and, for the model choice, quantile boosting instead of the Gaussian. At 15:00 it picks L2 1 instead of 3, and quantile boosting instead of the SHASH model the 2024 split preferred. None of the four test changes can be told apart from zero: −0.002 [−0.007, +0.002] and +0.011 [−0.010, +0.035] at 12:00, +0.001 [−0.0005, +0.003] and −0.004 [−0.014, +0.004] at 15:00. The early folds have only 346 training days and score worse (CURRENT 0.261 on 2022 against 0.187 on 2024 at 09:00), so the CV average mixes how hard each year was with how much data each fold had.
- **Regularization path.** Validation log loss changes little for L2 from 0.01 to 10: at 09:00 it falls from 0.195 to 0.187, and at 12:00 and 15:00 it moves by less than 0.001. It climbs steeply from L2 = 100 (0.233 at 09:00, reaching 0.505 at 1000) because the penalty shrinks the coefficients too far, which is underfitting. The overfitting side is shallow: with about 50 coefficients and 1040 days, the nearly unpenalized fit generalizes almost as well. At 15:00 the test score happens to be lowest at L2 = 100 (0.062), where validation says the penalty is too strong (0.097 against 0.082); we keep the validation choice.
- **Overfitting check.** CURRENT's train, validation and test log losses are close: 0.210 / 0.187 / 0.182 at 09:00, 0.173 / 0.169 / 0.136 at 12:00, and 0.077 / 0.082 / 0.070 at 15:00. The paper's selected systems (the two-classifier average at 09:00 and 12:00, XGBoost at 15:00) score 0.187 / 0.150 / 0.048 on training days against 0.252 / 0.232 / 0.111 on validation, a gap of 0.06 to 0.08. Distance-weighted KNN memorizes its training days (training log loss 0.003 to 0.004, validation 0.26 to 0.44). XGBoost and random forest have gaps of 0.05 to 0.12. Gaussian naive Bayes has a small gap but a large loss everywhere (0.28 to 0.80 on training days), the textbook high-bias case.
