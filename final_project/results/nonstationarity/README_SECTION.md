### Non-stationarity: drift is real, but handling it does not fix the validation/test disagreement

We checked whether changing forecast models and sensors explain why 2024 validation and the test year disagreed about long training windows (`nonstationarity.py`, figure `drift.png`, table `drift_table.csv`, data-detected breaks in `level_shifts.csv`). Breaks are the single largest shift in the deseasonalized monthly mean, and p values come from shuffling 3-month blocks. We mark version dates only for NBM, where NOAA documents the version changes; every other break below was found in the data, and we do not claim what caused it.
The guidance errors shift clearly. The GFS MOS daytime-max error drops by 1.3 F from January 2015 and the NAM MOS error by 2.1 F from August 2016 (both p < 0.001). The LAMP remaining-max error moves from -2.6 F in 2021 to -0.4 F in 2024, with a break of +1.7 F in June 2023 (p < 0.001) and a narrower spread afterwards. The HRRR error rises by 0.8 F from March 2022 (p = 0.007).
LGA itself also moved. LGA minus the mean of JFK, EWR and TEB, at the same cutoffs, rises by 0.6 F from January 2016 (p = 0.004), around the time both MOS errors fell. The sizes are consistent with a step change at LGA, but we did not identify the cause. The other single-neighbour breaks have opposite signs, so they look like changes at those stations.
NBM shows no data-detected break. After v5.0 (2026-05-04 to 08-31, compared with the same window in 2021-2025), the 09:00 NBM bias changes by +0.26 F (95% interval -0.30 to 0.82) and its SD by -0.23 F (-0.55 to 0.05). In the same window, GFS MOS shifts by +1.15 F and LAMP by +1.85 F, so part of the difference is the summer itself and not the version.
Handling drift with choices made on validation fails in a telling way. Recency weighting of the 2010-2023 MOS model improves 2024 validation (0.268 to 0.259 at 09:00 with a 2-year half-life, 0.198 to 0.186 at 12:00 with 0.5 years) but worsens the test year (0.203 to 0.219, 0.179 to 0.191). Weighting toward recent years helps 2024 and hurts 2025-26, so the disagreement is not a smooth drift that recency can track.
For CURRENT's inputs, validation picks a 0.5-year half-life at every cutoff. Its test log loss changes by +0.007 (-0.004 to 0.017), -0.001 (-0.013 to 0.013) and -0.003 (-0.006 to 0.001) at 09:00, 12:00 and 15:00.
Era indicators for the detected breaks leave the long model's validation flat and worsen its test score by 0.015 at 09:00. A LAMP indicator (before or after June 2023) in CURRENT's model improves test by 0.005 at 09:00 (0.002 to 0.009), but it is worse on validation, so it is not chosen. Year indicators hurt.
Across all fixed fits, validation selects the 0.5-year half-life model at 09:00 (test 0.2061 against CURRENT's 0.1995), a two-stage model with a recency-weighted stage 1 at 12:00 (0.1546 against 0.1591), and the unweighted two-stage model at 15:00 (0.0734 against 0.0722). No paired interval excludes zero.
Walk-forward monthly refitting is an operational result, not part of the protocol: it uses settled test-year labels from earlier months, with a 2-day lag. It is worse than the fixed fit on 2024 at every cutoff (0.212 / 0.174 / 0.099 against 0.211 / 0.171 / 0.098). In the test year it helps at 09:00 (-0.008, -0.015 to -0.001) and not at 12:00 or 15:00.
With an NBM v5 indicator added, the walk-forward 09:00 change against CURRENT is -0.018 (-0.029 to -0.008). The whole gain comes from the June to August 2026 refits and cannot be checked on validation. It may also absorb other changes in summer 2026.
We keep CURRENT. The drift is real and partly discrete (sensor 2016, LAMP 2023), but one validation year cannot tell which handling will carry forward.
