### Non-stationarity: drift is real, but handling it does not fix the validation/test disagreement

We checked whether changing forecast models and sensors explain why 2024 validation and the test year disagreed about long training windows (`nonstationarity.py`, figure `drift.png`, table `drift_table.csv`, data-detected breaks in `level_shifts.csv`). Breaks are the single largest shift in the deseasonalized monthly mean, and p values come from shuffling 3-month blocks. We mark version dates only for NBM, where NOAA documents the version changes; every other break below was found in the data, and we do not claim what caused it.
The guidance errors shift clearly. The GFS MOS daytime-max error drops by 1.3 F from January 2015 and the NAM MOS error by 2.1 F from August 2016 (both p < 0.001). The LAMP remaining-max error moves from -2.6 F in 2021 to -0.4 F in 2024, with a break of +1.7 F in June 2023 (p < 0.001) and a narrower spread afterwards. The HRRR error rises by 0.8 F from March 2022 (p = 0.008).
LGA itself also moved. LGA minus the mean of JFK, EWR and TEB, at the same cutoffs, rises by 0.6 F from January 2016 (p = 0.004), around the time both MOS errors fell. The sizes are consistent with a step change at LGA, but we did not identify the cause. The other single-neighbour breaks have opposite signs, so they look like changes at those stations.
NBM shows no data-detected break. After v5.0 (2026-05-04 to 08-31, compared with the same window in 2021-2025), the 09:00 NBM bias changes by +0.27 F (95% interval -0.29 to 0.82) and its SD by -0.24 F (-0.56 to 0.04). In the same window, GFS MOS shifts by +1.16 F and LAMP by +1.86 F, so part of the difference is the summer itself and not the version.
Handling drift with choices made on validation does not carry over to the test year. Recency weighting improves the 2010-2023 MOS model on 2024 at every cutoff (0.264 / 0.192 / 0.088 to 0.251 / 0.183 / 0.087 with half-lives of 2, 1 and 0.5 years). On test it is flat at 09:00 and 15:00 (+0.001, -0.000) and worse at 12:00 (0.164 to 0.171). Era indicators for the detected breaks give the same pattern: validation improves (0.264 to 0.252 at 09:00) and test does not (0.210 to 0.212 at 09:00, -0.002 / +0.001 at 12:00 / 15:00).
For CURRENT's inputs, validation picks a 0.5-year half-life at every cutoff. Its test log loss changes by -0.001 (-0.012 to 0.009), -0.005 (-0.019 to 0.011) and +0.001 (-0.004 to 0.007) at 09:00, 12:00 and 15:00.
A LAMP indicator (before or after June 2023) in CURRENT's model is worse on validation at 09:00 (0.197 against 0.187) and changes test by -0.003 (-0.009 to 0.004). Year indicators are close to neutral (-0.001 / -0.000 / -0.003, all intervals include zero).
Across all fixed fits, validation selects the 0.5-year half-life model at 09:00 (test 0.1809 against CURRENT's 0.1818) and at 12:00 (0.1318 against 0.1368). At 15:00 it selects a two-stage model with a 0.5-year recency-weighted stage 1 (0.0747 against 0.0701). The 15:00 pick is worse on test, with an interval that excludes zero (+0.0045, 0.0002 to 0.0097); the other two intervals include zero.
Walk-forward monthly refitting is an operational result, not part of the protocol: it uses settled test-year labels from earlier months, with a 2-day lag. Without decay it is worse than the fixed fit on 2024 at every cutoff (0.188 / 0.170 / 0.085 against 0.187 / 0.169 / 0.082). It does not help in the test year either: -0.002 / +0.006 / -0.003, all intervals including zero.
With a 1-year half-life, walk-forward beats the fixed fit on 2024 at 12:00 (0.168), so validation would choose it there, but it is worse on test (+0.016, -0.000 to 0.036). Adding an NBM v5 indicator to the walk-forward refits changes test by +0.002 / +0.008 / -0.004, so there is no measurable v5 effect to correct.
We keep CURRENT. The drift is real and partly discrete (sensor 2016, LAMP 2023), but no handling chosen on 2024 gives a reliable test gain, and one validation year cannot tell which would carry forward.
