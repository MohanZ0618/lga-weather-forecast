## Course ensembles and a neural network

`python final_project/class_models.py` (about one minute, offline, two threads; checks in `test_class_models.py`) tests the Lecture 11 and 13 models on CURRENT's inputs, its 1,040 training days (2021 to 2023) and the same 359 test days, with every choice made on 2024. Differences are paired by date against CURRENT's saved test probabilities with 7-day block bootstrap intervals; our refit of CURRENT reproduces them to within 0.0001 log loss at every cutoff.

**Bagging.** Averaging 50 censored Gaussians fit to bootstrap resamples of training weeks barely moves 2024 log loss (0.1868, 0.1677, 0.0815 against 0.1869, 0.1690, 0.0822 at 09:00, 12:00, 15:00) and lowers test log loss by only 0.0012, 0.0014 and 0.0005, with every interval including zero (for example [-0.0032, +0.0008] at 09:00).
The bias-variance view explains why: across bootstrap members the predicted mean warming moves by 0.31 to 0.45 F while the forecast spread is 1.6 to 1.8 F, so estimation variance is about 3 to 7% of the predictive variance and there is little for averaging to remove. Bagging pays off for high-variance learners such as deep trees, which is what a random forest exploits.

**Neural network.** A one-hidden-layer tanh network outputs the mean and log spread of the same censored Gaussian and is trained by minibatch Adam on the censored likelihood, with hand-written backpropagation (checked against finite differences), L2 weight decay and early stopping on 2024. Validation chose width 16, 4 and 16 from 24 settings (a linear input-to-output path only at 15:00) and stopped after 5 to 105 epochs.
Averaging five seeds lowered 2024 log loss in all 72 setting and cutoff cells (by 0.0003 to 0.0096), the variance reduction that bagging could not deliver for the linear model. The network is worse than CURRENT on 2024 at 09:00 (0.1934) and better at 12:00 and 15:00 (0.1594, 0.0714); on test it scores 0.1749, 0.1437 and 0.0724, and every paired interval includes zero (-0.0069 [-0.0256, +0.0119], +0.0069 [-0.0070, +0.0217], +0.0023 [-0.0049, +0.0111]).

**Stacking.** The paper's classifier families refit on the same inputs select an average of a random forest and XGBoost (boosting) at 09:00 and 15:00 and XGBoost alone at 12:00, and lose clearly (test 0.2327, 0.2090, 0.0951, every interval above zero); 2024 gives them weight 0.00, 0.15 and 0.10 in a pool with CURRENT, and the equal-weight pool is worse on both splits. Pooling CURRENT with the network, 2024 puts weight 0.85, 0.00 and 0.00 on CURRENT.

**What validation selects.** With fitted pools scored by 2-fold cross-fitting inside 2024, validation picks the bagged model at 09:00 (by 0.0001) and the network alone at 12:00 and 15:00. On test those picks score 0.1807, 0.1437 and 0.0724 against CURRENT's 0.1818, 0.1368 and 0.0701 (-0.0012 [-0.0032, +0.0008], +0.0069 [-0.0070, +0.0217], +0.0023 [-0.0049, +0.0111]), so nothing in this lane beats CURRENT beyond noise, and the network picks are worse on test.
The 2024 gains behind the network switches were -0.0096 [-0.0244, +0.0046] at 12:00 and -0.0108 [-0.0199, -0.0022] at 15:00, but they are optimistic because early stopping and the network's settings used the same days. Even a rule that switches only when the 2024 interval excludes zero would move 15:00 to the network, which then does not hold up on test. We therefore keep CURRENT.
