## Observed weather and nearby stations (IEM ASOS)

We downloaded routine and special METARs from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet for LGA, JFK, EWR, TEB and Central Park (NYC) for 2020 to 2026 and for HPN, ISP, FRG, CDW and SMQ for 2021 to 2026: 65 station-year files in 73 HTTP requests, counting retries after rate limiting (data/asos_sources.csv).
At each cutoff a report counts only if its valid time is strictly before the cutoff minus a 10-minute delivery allowance and at most 90 minutes older than the cutoff. Routine reports at these sites are taken at :51 to :56, so this rule drops the report from the hour just before the cutoff, and the newest usable routine report is 64 to 69 minutes old.
LGA's own weather (wind u and v, gust, altimeter and its 3 h change, dew point depression, sky cover, ceiling, 3 h precipitation) was added to CURRENT as (a). Temperature relative to LGA for the six new sites and sky cover for all nine other sites were added on top as (b). We fit the same censored Gaussian on the same 1,040 (09:00, 12:00) and 1,038 (15:00) complete 2021-2023 rows, filled missing values with the training median and chose L2 on 2024 from 1 to 300 (CURRENT keeps its own grid of 1, 10 and 30).
Neither helps reliably. Test log loss for (a) is 0.1820 / 0.1381 / 0.0672 and for (b) 0.1840 / 0.1355 / 0.0730 at 09:00 / 12:00 / 15:00, against 0.1818 / 0.1358 / 0.0701 for CURRENT on the same 359 days. Every paired 7-day block bootstrap interval for log loss includes zero. The largest gain is (a) at 15:00, -0.0029 (95% interval -0.0084 to +0.0032), and (b) at 12:00 changes log loss by only -0.0003. No accuracy interval for (a) or (b) excludes zero.
Validation does not support either gain. On 2024, (a) is worse than CURRENT at every cutoff (0.1876 / 0.1712 / 0.0842 against 0.1869 / 0.1693 / 0.0822). (b) is better only at 12:00, by 0.0007 (0.1685), and worse at 09:00 (0.1888) and 15:00 (0.0851). At 15:00 the wider L2 grid alone moves CURRENT from L2 = 1 to 3 (validation 0.0818, test -0.0012, interval -0.0029 to +0.0005), so part of every 15:00 difference comes from the penalty and not from the features.
Validation picks CURRENT plus LGA precipitation at 09:00, (b) without LGA clouds at 12:00 and CURRENT plus nearby sky cover at 15:00, with margins of 0.0002, 0.0036 and 0.0016. On test these change log loss by +0.0002, +0.0024 and -0.0007, and every interval includes zero. Margins this small are within selection noise across 18 candidates, so we keep CURRENT, the simpler model.
In the ablation, one add-one or drop-one interval excludes zero: CURRENT plus LGA clouds at 15:00, -0.0022 (-0.0046 to -0.0000). It shares the 15:00 grid shift above and is tied with CURRENT on validation (-0.0001). Dropping one group from (b) gains at most 0.0038 (nearby sky cover at 15:00, interval -0.0099 to +0.0023).
The 10-minute rule is not what hides a gain. With a 5-minute allowance, which keeps the :51 report as the paper's own station features do, (a) scores 0.1834 / 0.1381 / 0.0694 and (b) 0.1856 / 0.1349 / 0.0690, and every interval includes zero. (a) at 09:00 has the best 09:00 validation score of any system (0.1846), yet it is worse on test (+0.0015, interval -0.0006 to +0.0036).
Without guidance the features carry signal, but it is not consistent. In an observations-only model without HRRR, LAMP or NBM, adding (a) improves 09:00 test log loss from 0.3484 to 0.3280 (-0.0204, interval -0.0322 to -0.0085). At 12:00 it worsens test log loss from 0.1949 to 0.2047 (+0.0098, interval +0.0014 to +0.0190), even though validation favoured it there (-0.0057), and at 15:00 it changes nothing reliable.
Our reading is that at lead times of 2 to 15 hours, the guidance already carries most of what the latest METAR says about wind, cloud and moisture. Own-station weather pays off mainly in minute-scale nowcasting, where no forecast is available. Results are in results/observed_weather/.
