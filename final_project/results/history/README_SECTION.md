## Longer history and a skewed distribution

`python final_project/history.py` downloads LCD v2 for 2010–2018 (36 files, about 340 MB, gitignored) and builds 2010–2017 samples with `prepare.py`'s own functions into `data/history/`. Rebuilt 2018 rows equal the bundled samples in every cell (`data/history/overlap_check_2018.csv`), and the 2018 source files match `station_sources.csv` byte for byte. `python final_project/history_study.py` (about 70 seconds, offline) then tests two research ideas on the same 359 test days, choosing on 2024 validation:

- **Longer training windows.** The guidance model trained on 2018–2023 or 2010–2023 has to impute LAMP, NBM and HRRR before they existed (flags mark those rows). Test log loss rises from 0.200 / 0.157 / 0.072 (2021–2023, complete rows) to 0.251 / 0.173 / 0.080 (2018–2023) and 0.272 / 0.184 / 0.083 (2010–2023), all worse beyond the 95% interval: one set of coefficients cannot serve days with and without guidance. Where inputs exist in every year (observations only), 2010–2023 versus 2018–2023 changes the censored Gaussian by −0.004 / −0.002 / −0.001 (intervals include zero) and helps only quantile boosting at 09:00 (−0.021, interval below zero), which still trails the guidance models.
- **Sinh-arcsinh (SHASH) shape** (Jones and Pewsey 2009; `shash.py`): skew and tail weight as extra parameters, with the censored Gaussian as the special case. The fit finds heavier-than-normal tails (tail parameter 0.6–0.7), but test log loss is worse than the Gaussian at every cutoff (+0.004, +0.005, +0.007). Validation prefers it only at 15:00; that pick still beats the paper (0.079 against 0.094) but loses to the simpler Gaussian.

The selected system therefore stays the censored Gaussian with HRRR plus LAMP/NBM on 2021–2023. Its 80% intervals for the final high contain the outcome on 78.6%, 80.2% and 91.6% of test days at 09:00, 12:00 and 15:00. `test_history.py` checks the SHASH gradient against finite differences, parameter recovery on simulated data and the 2018 overlap.
